Control your Mac with your AirPods.

Point with your head. Click with a tap on an earbud, or with a sound. Or slide the case across a desk like a mouse.

Version 0.1.0 · macOS 14 or newer · Apple silicon · early release, see status

What it does

◎

Head mouse

Where you face is where the cursor goes. Set up once per pair of AirPods by following a few glowing targets, then tune horizontal and vertical sensitivity separately.

☝

Tap to click

One tap clicks, two double-click, three start a scroll mode for reading pages. The cursor is corrected for the knock, so it doesn't jump.

♫

Sound click

Teach it a sound: a short one clicks, two double-click, a long one holds the button. It listens on the Mac, never on your AirPods' mic.

▭

Case mouse

Slide an AirPod, or the case with the buds in it, across a table to move the cursor. A 3D case on screen opens and closes its lid as you stop and move.

●

Floating bot

A small bot head on your screen. Rest the cursor on it to turn PodsHub on or off, or let it open into four buttons: recentre, open, sound, off.

☀

Yours, per AirPods

Each pair keeps its own setup and name. Four themes: blue, pink, white and dark. Say “pod” for Apple Dictation, “pod close” to switch off.

Install

1. Open it

  1. Open the .dmg and drag PodsHub to Applications.
  2. The app is signed with a development certificate and is not notarized, so the first time: right-click it and choose Open.
  3. Or, in Terminal: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/PodsHub.app

2. Allow what it needs

  • Motion & Fitness: head and earbud motion.
  • Accessibility: moving the mouse, clicking, starting dictation.
  • Microphone and Speech Recognition: only for sound click and “pod”. Speech is recognised on your Mac only.

Set the AirPods as your Mac's sound output, then press Full recentre once. Turn PodsHub on with ⌃⌥P (Control + Option + P), or from the ON/OFF button.

Honest status

  • This is an early version built for one person's Mac. The logic has 51 automated tests, but taps, the case mouse and the sound features haven't been tested widely on real hardware.
  • macOS streams motion from one earbud at a time; the floating bot shows an L or R for the ear to tap.
  • The case mouse works from an accelerometer, so it is rough: good for short slides, not for slow, precise movement.
  • The 3D case is a simple stand-in model, not a photo-real one.
